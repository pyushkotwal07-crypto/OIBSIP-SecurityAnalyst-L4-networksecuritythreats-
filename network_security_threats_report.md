## 1. Introduction

Network security protects computer networks, devices, and data from unauthorized access, misuse, and cyberattacks. This report discusses three common threats—Denial of Service (DoS/DDoS), Man-in-the-Middle (MITM), and spoofing—along with their impact and prevention.

## 2. Objectives

- Understand common network security threats.
- Describe how these threats affect individuals and organizations.
- Identify defensive measures and secure networking practices.

## 3. Denial of Service (DoS) and Distributed Denial of Service (DDoS)

### Description
A Denial of Service attack attempts to make a website, server, or network service unavailable to legitimate users by overwhelming its resources or exploiting weaknesses. A Distributed Denial of Service attack involves traffic or requests from multiple systems.

### Impact
- Service interruptions and downtime.
- Financial losses and reduced productivity.
- Additional recovery and mitigation costs.

### Prevention
- Use firewalls and reputable DDoS protection services.
- Apply traffic filtering and rate limiting.
- Monitor traffic for unusual patterns.
- Maintain an incident response and recovery plan.

## 4. Man-in-the-Middle (MITM) Attack

### Description
A Man-in-the-Middle attack occurs when an unauthorized party intercepts or potentially alters communication between two parties who believe they are communicating directly.

### Impact
- Exposure of confidential information.
- Unauthorized alteration of transmitted data.
- Potential theft of credentials or session information.
- Loss of privacy and trust.

### Prevention
- Use HTTPS and properly configured TLS.
- Verify website certificates and heed browser security warnings.
- Use multi-factor authentication.
- Keep operating systems, browsers, and network devices updated.
- Avoid sending sensitive information over untrusted connections.

## 5. Spoofing

### Description
Spoofing falsifies identifying information to impersonate a trusted device, user, website, or communication source. Examples include IP spoofing, email spoofing, and DNS spoofing.

### Impact
- Unauthorized access attempts.
- Redirection to fraudulent websites.
- Delivery of phishing messages.
- Confusion about the identity of a communicating system.

### Prevention
- Configure appropriate network filtering.
- Use email authentication controls such as SPF, DKIM, and DMARC.
- Verify unexpected messages and requests independently.
- Use secure DNS services and monitoring.
- Apply strong authentication and access controls.

## 6. Comparison of Threats

| Threat | Main Concern | Key Defensive Measure |
|---|---|---|
| DoS/DDoS | Service availability | Traffic filtering and DDoS protection |
| MITM | Confidentiality and integrity of communication | HTTPS/TLS and certificate validation |
| Spoofing | False identity or source information | Authentication and filtering |

## 7. General Network Security Best Practices

1. Keep software and firmware updated.
2. Use strong, unique passwords and multi-factor authentication.
3. Configure firewalls and access permissions appropriately.
4. Monitor logs and network traffic.
5. Encrypt sensitive information during transmission.
6. Back up important data regularly.
7. Train users to recognize phishing and suspicious activity.
8. Maintain a documented incident response plan.

## 8. Conclusion

DoS/DDoS, MITM, and spoofing can threaten the availability, confidentiality, and integrity of network systems. No single measure prevents every attack, but secure communication, access controls, traffic monitoring, regular updates, and user awareness can significantly reduce risk.
